Labour Contract vs Turnkey House Construction

Compare labour-only and turnkey house construction contracts. Check material buying, supervision, exclusions and payments before choosing a builder.

By Garuda Construction · 17 November 2026

A labour-only construction quote and a turnkey quote divide responsibilities differently. Before comparing the total, write down who buys each material, who supervises the work, and who coordinates specialist trades. Two quotes become easier to compare once those responsibilities match your expectations.

For a homeowner building in Thiruvallur while working elsewhere, time spent arranging deliveries can matter alongside the price. An owner who already has a procurement team may make a different choice. Ask each builder to explain the arrangement using the same drawings and material schedule.

How the two contract types usually work

Confirm these responsibilities in the proposed agreement
ResponsibilityLabour-only arrangementTurnkey arrangement
MaterialsThe owner usually arranges materials. Confirm handling, storage and wastage.The builder supplies the materials listed in the agreed specifications.
Trades and supervisionConfirm which trades the labour contractor includes and who coordinates the others.Confirm the builder’s responsibility for coordination, site checks and specialist work.
Design and permissionsAgree separately who prepares drawings and coordinates submissions.Check which design and coordination work is included and which fees are separate.
ChangesNew materials and additional labour can both affect the cost.Changes to the agreed drawings or allowances need a priced variation.
PaymentMaterials, labour and specialist bills may follow different schedules.Payments commonly follow the milestones set out in the agreement.

The contract name gives you a starting point. The written scope determines the actual arrangement. Ask the builder to identify any owner-supplied items and any activities that require another contractor.

Compare one bathroom before comparing the whole house

Use a bathroom as a small worked example. List demolition or preparation, plumbing, waterproofing, wall and floor tiles, sanitary fittings, electrical points and installation. Ask both bidders to show the same quantities, material allowances and responsibilities for testing.

If one quote includes installation but leaves the owner to supply tiles, add those tile purchases to your comparison. If sanitary fittings have an allowance, ask how the final selection affects the price. This process also works for doors, windows, kitchen counters and painting.

Questions to answer before signing

  • Area measurement. Ask how the builder counts stairs, balconies, covered parking and terrace structures.
  • Specification. Record material brands, grades, sizes and allowances where they affect the offer.
  • Site responsibilities. Agree on temporary power, water, storage, security and debris removal.
  • Progress checks. Set out what completes each payment milestone and how you can review the work.
  • Changes and handover. Record how variations are approved, how remaining defects are listed and what warranty terms apply.

When should you compare prices?

Compare prices after the floor areas and scope are sufficiently defined. An early budget can help you choose a direction, while a drawing-based quote lets the builder account for your requirements. Keep a record of the drawings used for each quote so later changes are easy to identify.
