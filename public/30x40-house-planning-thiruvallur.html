Planning a House on a 30×40 Plot in Thiruvallur

Prepare a 30×40 house planning brief for Thiruvallur. Compare room priorities, parking, stairs, ventilation and future needs before commissioning drawings.

By Garuda Construction · 17 November 2026

A 30×40 ft plot has a land area of 1,200 sq ft. Start your house planning by noting which edge faces the road, how many people will live there, and what you need on each floor. Your architect can then assess the site and prepare a layout for that brief.

Two plots with the same dimensions can need different layouts because access, orientation, surrounding buildings and applicable planning requirements differ. A good first meeting turns your wish list into clear priorities that can be explored in drawings.

Build a room brief before choosing a layout

Questions to take to your architect
RequirementDecision to recordWhy it affects the design
BedroomsHow many bedrooms are needed now, and who uses each one?A ground-floor bedroom may be a priority for an older family member.
ParkingRecord the vehicles, their dimensions and how the gate will be used.Parking and pedestrian access share the frontage.
StairsWill the upper floor belong to the same household or have separate access?Access affects privacy, circulation and the entrance arrangement.
Kitchen and utilityList appliances, storage needs and washing activities.These decisions affect plumbing, electrical points and usable space.
Work and visitorsDecide how much privacy a study or guest space needs.Some activities can share space while others need a separate room.
Future needsDiscuss expected family changes and any proposed later construction.The architect and structural engineer need that brief at the design stage.

Compare two layout directions

For a single household using both floors, ask how the living area, stairs and bedrooms connect during a normal day. Walk through arriving with shopping, receiving visitors and moving between floors. This helps reveal inconvenient routes before work begins.

For separate upper-floor use, discuss the entrance, stair position, service access and privacy of the ground-floor rooms. Ask your design team to explain how those choices affect the overall building and its approvals. Record the intended use clearly from the start.

Give daylight and ventilation a place in the discussion

Take photos of the adjoining buildings and road-facing side. Tell the architect where you spend most time during the day and which rooms need privacy. Ask them to explain the proposed openings, shading and airflow using the actual site and surrounding context.

If Vastu preferences matter to your family, list your priorities together before the first design review. The designer can discuss them alongside room use, circulation, access and site constraints. Agree on the priorities before requesting detailed revisions.

Which drawings do you need next?

A concept plan helps you review the arrangement of spaces. Working drawings give the construction team the dimensions and coordinated information needed for execution. Ask which architectural, structural and service drawings are part of your agreed design scope and when each will be issued.

Confirm the current permission route for your exact site with the professional handling the submission. The Tamil Nadu Directorate of Town Panchayats links to the state’s online building permission system. Your architect should verify the authority and requirements applicable to the plot.

Take the agreed design into your budget

Ask for floor-by-floor built-up areas before obtaining construction quotes. The G+1 construction estimate example shows how area affects a budget, while the Thiruvallur cost guide uses Garuda’s published package rates. Keep the area assumptions with each estimate.

Review your plot with the design team

Bring dimensions, site photos, your room brief and any existing drawings. We can discuss the next steps for a site-specific design through our architectural planning service.
